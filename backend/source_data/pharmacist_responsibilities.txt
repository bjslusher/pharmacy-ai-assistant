Pharmacist Corresponding Responsibility and DEA Compliance Essentials (Educational Summary)

DISCLAIMER: Educational only. See 21 CFR 1306.04 and the current DEA Pharmacist's Manual. Not legal advice.

Corresponding Responsibility (21 CFR 1306.04):
A prescription for a controlled substance must be issued for a legitimate medical purpose by an individual practitioner acting in the usual course of professional practice. The pharmacist who fills the prescription has a corresponding responsibility. Knowingly filling a prescription not issued in the usual course of professional treatment may subject the pharmacist to penalties and administrative action.

Red-flag examples that may require further inquiry:
- Unusually large quantities or high doses without clear medical justification
- Early refill requests
- Cash payments for controlled substances when insurance is available
- Patients traveling long distances or using multiple practitioners
- Altered or forged-appearing prescriptions
- Patterns of the same controlled substances from one prescriber to many patients

Actions:
- Exercise professional judgment. The law does not require a pharmacist to dispense a prescription of doubtful origin.
- Contact the prescriber when red flags appear.
- Use state Prescription Drug Monitoring Programs (PDMP) as required or as best practice.
- Document efforts and decisions.
- Report suspected diversion when warranted.

Recordkeeping (federal minimum):
- Maintain records of controlled substances received, dispensed, and inventoried (at least 2 years; states may require longer).
- Biennial inventory required.
- Schedule II records often kept separately.

Registration:
- Pharmacies must hold a current DEA registration to handle controlled substances.
- Practitioners must have a valid DEA number.

This summary is educational only. Consult the current DEA Pharmacist's Manual, 21 CFR Parts 1300-1316, and applicable state pharmacy laws for authoritative guidance.
