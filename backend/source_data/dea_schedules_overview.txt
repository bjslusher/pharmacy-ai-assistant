DEA Controlled Substances Schedules Overview (Educational Summary based on CSA / 21 CFR 1308 and public DEA materials)

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
This is an educational summary only. Always consult the current DEA Pharmacist's Manual, 21 CFR Part 1308, and state law for authoritative requirements. Not legal or clinical advice.

The Controlled Substances Act places drugs into five schedules based on medical use, abuse potential, and dependence liability.

SCHEDULE I
- High potential for abuse
- No currently accepted medical use in treatment in the United States
- Lack of accepted safety for use under medical supervision
- May NOT be prescribed, administered, or dispensed for medical use
Examples: heroin, LSD, marijuana (cannabis at federal level), MDMA (ecstasy), methaqualone, peyote

SCHEDULE II
- High potential for abuse which may lead to severe psychological or physical dependence
- Currently accepted medical use (sometimes with severe restrictions)
- Prescriptions generally cannot be refilled; new prescription required
Examples: oxycodone (OxyContin, Percocet), hydrocodone combination products, fentanyl, morphine, methadone, amphetamine (Adderall), methylphenidate (Ritalin, Concerta)

SCHEDULE III
- Potential for abuse less than Schedules I and II
- Currently accepted medical use
- Abuse may lead to moderate or low physical dependence or high psychological dependence
- May be refilled up to 5 times in 6 months if authorized
Examples: products containing not more than 90 mg of codeine per dosage unit (Tylenol with Codeine), ketamine, anabolic steroids, buprenorphine products

SCHEDULE IV
- Low potential for abuse relative to Schedule III
- Currently accepted medical use
- Limited physical or psychological dependence relative to Schedule III
- Refills up to 5 times in 6 months if authorized
Examples: alprazolam (Xanax), diazepam (Valium), lorazepam (Ativan), clonazepam, zolpidem (Ambien), tramadol

SCHEDULE V
- Low potential for abuse relative to Schedule IV
- Currently accepted medical use
- Limited quantities of certain narcotics
Examples: cough preparations containing not more than 200 mg of codeine per 100 ml or per 100 grams (Robitussin AC)

PHARMACIST CORRESPONDING RESPONSIBILITY
Under 21 CFR 1306.04 the pharmacist shares corresponding responsibility with the prescribing practitioner. A prescription must be issued for a legitimate medical purpose by a practitioner acting in the usual course of professional practice. Pharmacists must not dispense prescriptions of doubtful, questionable, or suspicious origin.
